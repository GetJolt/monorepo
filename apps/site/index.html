Every platform gets worse once it needs to please investors. Jolt can't.

It's a familiar story. A chat app wins over communities by being great, then slowly starts working for someone else: ads in your sidebar, features moved behind a subscription, rules you never agreed to. Jolt is open source and anyone can run it, so there's no single company that can do that to you.

Familiar where it counts. Different where it matters.

  1. Servers from anywhere. The rail holds servers from every instance you've joined, side by side.
  2. Channels, categories, unread. Organise conversation the way your community already does.
  3. Neighbours, not strangers. People from other instances show up with their home address.
  4. Invites that travel. Paste a link from any instance and it becomes a card you can join from.
  5. Roles & presence. Colour-coded roles, real permissions, and who's around right now.

A timeline in order, from people you chose.

Servers are for your communities. The timeline is for everyone else: the friend on another instance, the artist on Mastodon, the project you want to keep up with. It lives right next to your servers in the same app, under the same account.

Posts are stored by the instance you signed up on and sent straight to your followers' servers. There's no central feed to rank them and no one deciding what goes viral.

Bring your other accounts Link Bluesky and Mastodon to show them on your profile with a verified badge, post to them in one go, read and reply to their timelines without leaving Jolt, and find the people you already follow there.

Your home instance vouches for you. That's it.

  1. Step 1 / 3

    Sign in at home

    Your account lives on one instance. It signs a short-lived certificate for this device.

  2. Step 2 / 3

    Show it anywhere

    Another instance checks the certificate against your home's public key. No new account needed.

  3. Step 3 / 3

    Talk where the server lives

    Messages stay on the instance that hosts the server. Nothing is copied around the network.

Six promises, in writing.

  1. Your server, your rules.

    Every instance is run by the people who use it, with its own moderation and policies.

  2. No ads. No tracking.

    Nothing to sell, so nothing to watch. Conversations are not a product.

  3. One account, every instance.

    Join servers anywhere with the address you already have.

  4. Built for everyone.

    Full keyboard control, screen reader support, high contrast and scalable text from day one.

  5. Open all the way down.

    Server, protocol, SDK and apps. Read it, audit it, fork it.

  6. Small enough to own.

    One process and one database file. A spare computer will do.

An instance in three lines.

  1. Point a domain at a machine with Docker.

  2. Set your domain.

    echo JOLT_DOMAIN=chat.example.org > .env

  3. Start it. HTTPS is handled for you.

    docker compose up -d

The app is a thin layer. Build your own.

Sign-in, federation, the live store, optimistic messages and read markers all live in @getjolt/sdk. The desktop app is just one client. A web app, a bot or something nobody has thought of yet can use exactly the same foundations.
