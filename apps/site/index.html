Group chat you can run yourself.

Jolt is a free, open-source chat app with the servers, channels and roles you're used to. The twist is that anyone can host a Jolt server, and they all link up, so one account gets you into every one of them. No ads, and nothing locked behind a subscription.

Download Jolt Open in your browser

Windows and Linux for now, macOS is on the way. Want your own server? Host one.

If you've used Discord, you'll feel at home

Servers down the side, channels and categories, roles with proper permissions, invites, unread markers and who's online. People from other Jolt servers show up right alongside everyone else, with their home server next to their name so you know where they're from.

It's also built to be usable entirely from the keyboard and with a screen reader. If something doesn't work for you, that's a bug.

Plus a timeline, minus the algorithm

Next to your servers there's a feed for following people, a bit like Bluesky or Mastodon. Posts show up newest first from the people you follow. Nothing gets boosted and nothing gets buried.

Jolt speaks ActivityPub, so people on Mastodon and the rest of the fediverse can follow you, and you can follow them back.

You can also link your Bluesky and Mastodon accounts. They show up on your profile with a verified badge, you can post to them at the same time, and you can read and reply to their timelines without leaving Jolt.

How one account works everywhere

  1. You sign in at home

    Home is wherever you made your account, like joltapp.org. It gives your device a short-lived pass that proves it's you.

  2. Other servers check the pass

    Join a server hosted somewhere else and it checks your pass against your home server's public key. No second account.

  3. Chat stays where it lives

    Messages live on the server that hosts the chat. They don't get copied around the network.

Why make another chat app?

Chat apps tend to start out great and then get worse once they need to make money. Ads creep in, bigger uploads turn into a subscription perk, and the rules change under you. Jolt doesn't have a company in the middle that could do that. The code is open, anyone can run a server, and each server sets its own rules.

  • No ads, and no tracking to sell them with.
  • Upload limits are up to each server, not a paid tier.
  • The server, protocol, SDK and apps are all open source.

Host your own

You need a domain and a machine with Docker. A spare PC is plenty. It runs on SQLite out of the box and can move to Postgres if your server grows.

Build your own client

The desktop app sits on top of @getjolt/sdk, which handles signing in, federation, live updates and the rest. Use it for a web client, a bot, or whatever you've got in mind.
